every file in `scripts/`, `logs/` and `results/`, plus the datasets and operator logs that are too large for git.

## Questions to expect

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Why is the degradation in the operator, not the recorded data? | Corrupting recorded data afterwards would make "bad data" a labeling trick. Degrading what the operator sees changes the actions it records, which is how real teleoperation failures happen. |
| Why did you change K from 0.3 to 0.2? | At 0.3 the 250 ms condition is past the correction loop's stability limit and almost every attempt fails (12%). At 0.2 quality degrades gradually, which is what a quality score must rank. Both batches are reported. |
| Jitter 15 mm did better than 5 mm? | Within sampling noise (intervals overlap); the operator's gradual correction filters most perception noise. Reported as a weak factor, not hidden. |
| How do you know the labels are right? | They reproduce NVIDIA's own labels on its 5 seeds (poses to 5e-7, boundaries within ±4 steps), and Mimic successfully amplified a labeled operator demo (13/15). |
